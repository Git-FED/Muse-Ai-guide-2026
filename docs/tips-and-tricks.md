# Muse Tips and Tricks

Practical beginner guidance based on the [official Muse getting-started guide](https://www.meta.com/help/artificial-intelligence/1331373868832401/), [Muse design overview](https://introducing.muse.ai/), and [Muse safety overview](https://security.muse.ai/).

## 1. Start with an outcome, not a vague topic

Tell Muse what you want to be true when the task is finished. Add the audience, deadline, constraints, and what a good result looks like.

> “Create a one-page study plan for a beginner, using 30-minute sessions, with a checklist at the end.”

## 2. Add the context that changes the answer

Relevant detail improves the result. Include the background, source material, preferences, examples, and restrictions that matter. You do not need to repeat context that Muse already has in the conversation.

## 3. Ask for the format you actually need

Say whether you want a short answer, table, checklist, email draft, document, spreadsheet, image, or another artifact. A format request often improves usefulness more than adding extra adjectives.

## 4. Use follow-ups instead of restarting

Muse is designed for ongoing conversations. Say what to keep, what to change, and why. Try: “Keep the structure, make it shorter, add two examples, and explain the changes.”

## 5. Use side chats for separate projects

Create a side chat when a topic needs its own context or would clutter the main conversation. Keep one side chat per durable project, course, or recurring workflow.

## 6. Give boundaries explicitly

State what Muse may and may not use or do. For example: “Only search the attached files,” “Do not send anything,” or “Read email but do not reply.” Narrow boundaries make tool use easier to review.

## 7. Ask for a plan before a complex task

For multi-step work, ask Muse to summarize the plan, assumptions, tools, and approval points before it starts. Check the plan before allowing an external or state-changing action.

## 8. Review activity, permissions, and goals

Muse can work in the background and track longer-running goals. Check its activity and permission views, confirm what it is doing, and change or disconnect access that is no longer needed.

## 9. Treat important output as a draft

Muse may be inaccurate or take unexpected actions. Verify claims, inspect files and links, and compare the result with your original goal before sharing, publishing, sending, buying, deleting, or changing anything.

## 10. Use artifacts when text is the wrong shape

Ask for a document, PDF, spreadsheet, image, dashboard, or other artifact when a long paragraph would be hard to use. Review the finished artifact in the Library and request revisions.

## 11. Set reminders with a clear time

Include the thing, date, time, timezone when relevant, and any useful preparation. Confirm that the reminder details are correct after Muse creates it.

## 12. Customize Muse deliberately

You can adjust Muse’s name, personality, and preferences. Make changes that improve clarity and usefulness, then record stable preferences in your guide or memory files.

## 13. Keep connected services narrow

Connect only services you need. Start with the smallest permission scope, understand what data moves between services, and disconnect integrations that are no longer useful.

## 14. Keep a human in the loop for consequential actions

Use approval for sending communications, publishing, purchases, financial actions, deleting data, or changing system configuration. A fast approval is better than an irreversible surprise.

## 15. Improve prompts with a simple revision loop

1. State the goal.
2. Add missing context.
3. Set constraints and format.
4. Ask for a draft or plan.
5. Inspect the result.
6. Give precise revision instructions.
7. Verify the final output.

## Quick prompt template

```text
Goal: [what I want finished]
Context: [background and relevant inputs]
Constraints: [limits, boundaries, tone, deadline]
Output: [format and length]
Approval: [what Muse must ask before doing]
Verification: [how I will check the result]
```
